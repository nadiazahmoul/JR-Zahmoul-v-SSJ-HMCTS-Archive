CONSOLIDATED ACCOUNTABILITY RECORD (CAR)
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Zahmoul v State / Courts / Systems (2021–Present)
Purpose
This Consolidated Accountability Record (“CAR”) brings together, in one public and chronological record, the procedural history, documentary evidence, and oversight correspondence arising from multiple interrelated proceedings involving a disabled litigant. It exists to prevent fragmentation, to enable scrutiny, and to show how cumulative procedural decisions have produced ongoing harm.
This record is not a pleading, not an appeal, and not an allegation of individual wrongdoing. It is a factual consolidation designed to allow institutions with oversight responsibility to see the whole picture.

Scope
The CAR covers:
•	Family proceedings and post-judgment implementation (including the Decree Absolute and appeal);
•	Disability status, participation measures, and Equality Act duties;
•	Administrative justice failures (enforcement, deflection of responsibility);
•	Evidence handling and safeguarding issues;
•	Public law proceedings (Judicial Review);
•	Parliamentary and ministerial oversight correspondence;
•	Appellate outcomes and identified non-engagement with disability issues.
The record spans 2021 to the present and is updated as proceedings continue.

Who this concerns
The record concerns a litigant in person with diagnosed autism and PTSD, whose ability to participate safely and effectively in litigation depends on clarity, predictability, and stable legal frameworks. These requirements were known to the courts and agencies involved.

Key contextual facts
•	A High Court made a final financial remedies order that included an express sequencing safeguard linking finality to proper implementation.
•	Following trial, participation safeguards weakened rather than strengthened.
•	Substantive obligations (including pensions, enforcement, and governance arrangements) remained unfulfilled or ambiguous.
•	Finality decisions were taken while participation failures persisted.
•	Judicial and administrative routes addressed matters in isolation, leaving no forum to assess cumulative harm.

What the consolidated record shows
Across proceedings and years, the documents demonstrate a pattern, not isolated error:
1.	Procedural activity continued during documented periods of medical incapacity, without effective adjustment or pause.
2.	Disability safeguards collapsed post-judgment, precisely when clarity and structure were most required.
3.	Implementation mechanisms were left ambiguous or informal, despite known vulnerability to uncertainty.
4.	Responsibility was repeatedly deflected between courts and agencies (“take it up with the respondent”), leaving no effective remedy.
5.	Finality was used to remove supervision while substantive obligations remained outstanding.
6.	Appellate decisions did not engage with Equality Act duties, participation failures, or cumulative procedural irregularities raised.
7.	Fragmentation obscured harm by preventing any single body from seeing the full sequence.

Why consolidation is necessary
Fragmentation benefits opacity. Consolidation enables accountability.
Without a single, longitudinal record:
•	each court or agency can treat its decision as discrete;
•	cumulative effects on a disabled litigant are not assessed;
•	oversight bodies lack the evidence base needed to intervene.
The CAR exists to ensure that decisions are understood in context, not in isolation.



Relationship to ongoing proceedings
The CAR does not ask courts to determine matters outside their jurisdiction. It provides context only. For example, the Decree Absolute appeal seeks protective, sequencing-based relief because finality is unsafe in the context documented here.

Oversight and public interest
Judicial immunity and procedural silos limit the capacity of courts to address systemic patterns. Parliamentary oversight exists precisely to examine such issues.
This record is therefore maintained in the public interest so that:
•	oversight bodies can assess whether inquiry thresholds are met;
•	disabled court users’ experiences can be understood longitudinally;
•	transparency replaces secrecy where harm is ongoing.

Status
This CAR is a living record. Documents are added as proceedings progress. The structure is fixed; the evidence base grows.
